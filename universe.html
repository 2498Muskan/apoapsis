Planet I · Closest to the Sun

Mercury

A world with almost no atmosphere to hold onto heat — daytime scorches past 400°C while night falls to -180°C. It's the fastest planet in the solar system, circling the Sun in just 88 days.

Planet II · The Morning Star

Venus

Thick clouds of sulfuric acid trap heat so effectively that Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system — hotter even than Mercury. It also spins backwards, and its day is longer than its year.

Planet III · The Blue Planet

Earth

The only known ocean planet — water covers 70% of the surface. Earth's tilted axis gives us seasons, and the Moon's pull steadies that tilt and slows our spin, one leap second at a time.

Planet IV · The Red Planet

Mars

Iron oxide dust gives Mars its rust-red color. It's home to Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system, and Valles Marineris, a canyon system that would stretch across the continental US.

Planet V · The Gas Giant

Jupiter

The largest planet in the solar system, wrapped in bands of swirling gas. Its Great Red Spot is a storm bigger than Earth that has raged for centuries. Jupiter's gravity shields the inner planets from debris.

Planet VI · Lord of the Rings

Saturn

Famous for its dazzling rings of ice and rock, Saturn is the least dense planet in the solar system — it would float in water if there were an ocean big enough. Its rings span up to 282,000 km but are often less than a kilometer thick.

Planet VII · The Tilted Giant

Uranus

An ice giant that rolls around the Sun on its side, tilted almost 98° — likely the result of an ancient collision. Its pale blue-green color comes from methane in the atmosphere absorbing red light.

Planet VIII · The Windiest World

Neptune

The most distant planet, with storms reaching over 2,000 km/h — the fastest winds in the solar system. Neptune was the first planet found by mathematical prediction rather than direct observation.
